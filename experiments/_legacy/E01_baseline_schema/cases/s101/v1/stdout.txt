The number of solar flares increased gradually from February 2021 to February 2026, with the busiest month being February 2021. The share of strong flares (GOES flux ≥ 1e-6 W/m²) rose steadily from year to year, indicating a growing intensity of powerful flares. Monthly flare activity shows a consistent upward trend, especially in the latter half of the period. The peak in monthly counts occurred in February 2021, suggesting higher solar activity at that time. The increasing share of strong flares over the years indicates a shift toward more intense solar events. This pattern may reflect the progression of solar cycle activity or increased solar disturbances. No significant drop or plateau occurred during the period, suggesting sustained solar activity. The trend implies that monitoring strong flares is increasingly important for space weather forecasting.
